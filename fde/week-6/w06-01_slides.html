How can AI help a hospital code a visit?

The customer

A regional hospital and its outpatient clinics use Epic for clinical notes and medical coding.

Coders turn a doctor’s note into insurance billing codes. Errors can cause claim denials and rework.

Illustrative thread: Encounter A, one completed patient visit. No real patient data is shown.

The finished interaction

Doctor: Records the visit in Epic.

AI: Proposes diagnosis, procedure, and modifier codes with supporting note text.

Medical coder: Confirms or corrects the proposal.

Billing: Receives only confirmed codes.

This is a coding assistant for human review; the model does not send claims on its own.

What must the hospital agree before we build?

The coding task

ICD-10 identifies diagnoses.
CPT identifies procedures and services.
Modifiers add applicable detail.

The required boundary

Patient data stays in this hospital’s approved private environment.
Every proposal needs qualified coder confirmation.

The definition of success

Agree on the correct output, priority departments, scoring method, response time, and release owner.

Define a correct, usable proposal before choosing a model or a training method.

Define the test: Missing codes, unsupported codes, applicable modifiers, complete code sets, and results by specialty.

Claim acceptance and coding accuracy are different measures; do not treat these percentages as one scale.

How do we pilot inside the hospital workflow?

  1. Start with an agreed department

    Set quality and response-time criteria; keep every coder review.

  2. Validate the Epic connection with hospital IT

    Confirm supported interfaces, permissions, data handling, and the actual review screen.

  3. Assign owners and rollback

    Own training jobs, live inference, Epic connector, and audit store; retain the previous approved version.

A pilot must prove a usable review workflow and operational readiness, as well as model quality.

What should live corrections cause us to do?

Observe

Coder corrections · quality by specialty · latency and cost · claim outcomes

Diagnose

New department → revisit Discover
Changed rules → update rules or retrieval
Repeated behavior errors → consider new training

Release deliberately

Review new labels, retest changes, and approve a version before replacing the live model.

Corrections are evidence for the next improvement—not permission for automatic retraining.
