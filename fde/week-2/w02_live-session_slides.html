02-01

Measuring and Deciding

Crucial Step: Confirm the Target

Before running the demo, confirm with the customer that the success metric from the scope document is still the agreed standard to prevent moving goalposts.

  • Primary Metric: "We agreed the target was X, and here is what we measured."
  • Latency: Does it meet the operational requirements?
  • Cost per Transaction: Is it within budget at projected production volume?
  • Edge Cases: Where does the system underperform, and how often?

Decision Point

02-01

The Three Possible Outcomes

All three are valid. The sprint succeeds when it produces a clear one.

Proceed
The metric was met, integration works, and customer is confident. Agree on next steps before leaving the room.
Pivot
The metric was not met, but the sprint revealed a specific path. Define what needs to change before the next attempt.
Stop
The approach doesn't work, or the cost/latency makes it infeasible. A stop outcome is the correct answer produced at the lowest possible cost.

Handoff

02-01

The Readout Document

The readout document officially closes the sprint. It is a single page covering:

  • What was built.
  • What was measured.
  • What the measurement showed.
  • The recommendation (Proceed, Pivot, Stop).
A Proceed decision does not mean the POC output moves directly to production. Managing Expectations

The POC answered one question: Does this approach work? The next phase builds a complete version to handle real inputs and real users.

The Agreement

02-02

The Pilot Scope & Shadow Mode

The scope creates a shared standard for "done" and helps the FDE manage scope expansion mid-flight.

Scope Elements
Must define: Capabilities, Integrations, specific Users, Timeline, and Success Criteria (performance, adoption, trust).
Feedback Cadence
Establish a weekly or bi-weekly rhythm of user check-ins. A pilot with no structured check-ins produces feedback too late to act on.
Shadow Mode (Optional)
The AI processes live requests, but outputs are hidden from users. FDEs compare accuracy against the manual process before going live.

Success Signals

02-02

Evaluating Pilot Performance

Metric performance is only one part of the evaluation. Three signals determine readiness:

Reliability
Stable latency and predictable output. Average metrics can look strong while hiding concentrated failure patterns in specific input categories.
Adoption
Do users organize their work around the system's output? Low adoption with strong metrics usually points to a usability issue.
Trust
Do users act on the output, or do they re-verify everything independently? High re-verification signals a lack of confidence.

Deliverables

02-02

What the FDE Hands Off

1. Maintainable Code

  • Separates config from logic and avoids local dependencies.
  • Committed to a repo the customer team can access from day one.

2. The Live Session

  • An end-to-end walkthrough of architecture and monitoring.
  • Recorded so the team can refer back to it.

3. The Handoff Document

  • System Overview: What it does and doesn't handle.
  • Data Flow: Where data moves and what hits external APIs.
  • Failure Modes: How it fails and what triggers it.
  • Monitoring Setup: What each alert means operationally.
  • Escalation Path: When a human should intervene.
