Module 6 · Preview

What · The idea

An always-on agent keeps working when you’re not there.

Who starts it

A regular agent: you. An always-on agent: a time you set, or something that happens.

Are you there while it works?

A regular agent: yes. An always-on agent: usually not.

It comes back with the result, or with a decision it needs from you.

Many run in the cloud, so they keep going when your laptop is closed. Some run on your own computer, and those usually need it to stay on.

Why · What it can do

Some work needs to happen when you’re not there.

Here are three kinds. The example is a weekly sales report for your sales lead.

On a schedule

Every Monday at 8 a.m., the sales report is drafted and waiting for you.

When something happens

The sales lead emails asking for a change. A draft reply is ready for you to review.

Over several days

Each new week of sales comes in. The forecast gets updated, and you get the decisions.

You describe the job once. You don’t have to start it again every time.

Runs can start a little late. So it’s usually not the right tool for something that has to happen the moment it changes.

Step 1 · Before it runs

Step 1: Start with a low-risk job you already do.

Low risk means a mistake is easy to spot and easy to fix.

A job you already do

You’ve done it by hand at least once. You know what to ask for and what a good result looks like.

Our example

The agent drafts the Monday sales report. You still send it.

Module 6 · Preview

Step 2 · Before it runs

Step 2: Say what to do, when to do it, and when to tell you.

Fill in three blanks.

When

Every Monday at 8 a.m.

Do

Make the one-page sales report from the two weekly sheets.

Tell me if

A region drops more than 20%.

Only what needs you.
You say“Every Monday at 8 a.m. Eastern, make the one-page sales report from the two weekly sheets. Tell me when the draft is ready. Message me right away only if a region drops more than 20%.”

If you set a time, include your time zone.

Step 3 · Before it runs

Step 3: Decide ahead of time what it can do on its own.

It may run when you’re not there to answer. So you decide before it starts.

Level 3, only when it’s earned

Only for a routine step you’ve seen it do well a few times.

For the sales report

It reads the sheets and drafts the report. Sending it to the sales lead is your call.

Many tools also have a built-in safety check for risky actions. It helps, but it can miss things, so it doesn’t replace your own limits.

Module 6 · Preview

Step 4 · After each run

Step 4: Check every run, and pause what you don’t use.

1. Did it run at all?A run can be late or skipped. Most tools list past runs on a schedule or activity page.

2. Is the result right?Compare it with your source sheets and your list of what must be true.

Don’t need it anymore? Pause it or delete it.

Pausing stops what comes next. It usually doesn’t undo what the agent has already done.

When · Practice

Which agent fits?

Does it need to happen when you’re not there?No? A regular agent is enough.

Is it low-risk enough to run without you watching?No? Let it prepare the work, and do the last step yourself.
